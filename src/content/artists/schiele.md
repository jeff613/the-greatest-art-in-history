Egon Schiele was born in June 1890 in the station house at Tulln, a small town on the Danube where his father Adolf ran the railway. The boy drew trains obsessively; his father, at one point, is said to have destroyed the sketchbooks. Then Adolf's health broke down — the family later understood it as syphilis — and he died at the turn of 1905, leaving a fourteen-year-old who never really recovered from it and a household with no money. Guardianship passed to an uncle who thought the drawing was a waste of a boy and eventually gave up on him.

He was gifted enough that the Academy of Fine Arts in Vienna admitted him at sixteen, and stubborn enough to find it intolerable. His professor, Christian Griepenkerl, taught a dead nineteenth-century orthodoxy; Schiele lasted three years, then walked out in 1909 with a group of equally disgusted students and founded the Neukunstgruppe. By then he had already done the more useful thing, which was to knock on Gustav Klimt's door in 1907. Klimt — the reigning figure in Viennese art, twenty-eight years older, and generous with the young — bought his drawings, swapped work with him, sent models and collectors his way. Schiele took the license and threw away the gold. What he wanted was the body as it actually behaves: joints, tendons, mottled skin, hands that clench, people looking straight out of the picture without apology.

Vienna did not care for it, and the provinces cared even less. In 1911 he took up with Walburga Neuzil — Wally, seventeen, previously one of Klimt's models — and the two of them were run out of Krumau in Bohemia for living together unmarried. They moved to Neulengbach, west of Vienna, and in April 1912 the police arrived over a teenage runaway who had turned up at their house. The abduction and seduction charges collapsed under examination. What stuck was a conviction for keeping erotic drawings where children could see them: twenty-four days, most of it already served, and a judge who burned one of the confiscated sheets in open court.

In 1915 he married Edith Harms, from the respectable family across the street, and ended things with Wally by handing her a written proposal that they still holiday together each summer without his wife. She refused it and walked away for good; she died nursing in Dalmatia in 1917, aged twenty-three. Three days after the wedding the army took him, though it never sent him to the front.

Then 1918 gave him everything at once. Klimt died in February, and the Secession handed Schiele the main hall in March. The show sold, prices jumped, commissions poured in, and at twenty-eight he was the leading painter in Vienna. That October the influenza reached the city. Edith died on the 28th, six months pregnant. Schiele drew her face from her deathbed, and died himself three days later.
