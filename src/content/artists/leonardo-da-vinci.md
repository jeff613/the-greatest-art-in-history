He was born on the wrong side of the blanket, and it may have been the making of him. In 1452 a Florentine notary named Ser Piero fathered a son by a peasant woman called Caterina, and because the boy was illegitimate he was barred from his father's respectable profession and never given a proper Latin education. Leonardo grew up in the hill town of Vinci, taught himself by watching, and signed his notebooks for the rest of his life as an "unlettered man" — half boast, half wound. Shut out of the world of books, he went to the world itself.

Apprenticed in his teens to Andrea del Verrocchio, Florence's leading workshop, he learned to paint, cast bronze, and build machines. The legend, told by Vasari, is that Leonardo painted an angel into his master's picture so beautiful that Verrocchio put down his brush forever. In 1476 an anonymous note dropped into a Florentine denunciation box accused the young artist of sodomy; the case collapsed, but the fright may explain the guardedness that shadowed his whole life. Around 1482 he left for Milan and offered his services to Duke Ludovico Sforza in a famous letter that spends ten paragraphs on his skills as a builder of war machines and one modest line, almost an afterthought, on painting.

For seventeen years Milan was home. He painted The Last Supper and Cecilia Gallerani, staged pageants, designed a colossal bronze horse that was never cast, and filled thousands of notebook pages — written backwards, right to left, in mirror script — with studies of anatomy, water, flight, and the movement of a human smile. Then the French invaded, the duke fell, and Leonardo became a wanderer: Florence, where he began the Mona Lisa; the service of the warlord Cesare Borgia; Rome; and finally France, where King Francis I gave him a manor house at Amboise and, it is said, treated him as a living wonder.

He died there in 1519, leaving perhaps fifteen finished paintings — some of them abandoned, some he could never bear to call complete. It is an absurdly small body of work for the most famous name in art. But he had never really been a painter who happened to think; he was a mind that happened, now and then, to paint.
