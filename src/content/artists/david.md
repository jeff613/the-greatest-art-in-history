Jacques-Louis David was born in Paris in 1748 into a comfortable merchant family. His father was killed in a duel when the boy was nine, and the uncles who raised him wanted an architect; he wanted only to paint. He set his heart on the Prix de Rome, the prize that sent young artists to study the antiquities of Italy, and failed so many times that he is said to have tried to starve himself to death in despair before finally winning it in 1774.

Rome remade him. Among its ruins and marbles he shed the frothy, pastel manner of the age of Fragonard and forged a severe new style built on ancient models — clean, sculptural, morally charged. When he unveiled the Oath of the Horatii in 1784, it landed like a thunderclap, and David became the most talked-about painter in France.

Then the Revolution arrived, and he threw himself into it body and soul. A friend of Robespierre and a fervent Jacobin, he was elected to the National Convention, where in 1793 he voted for the death of King Louis XVI. He became the pageant-master of the new republic, staging its festivals, designing its emblems, and painting its martyrs — above all his murdered friend Marat. For a moment he was something close to an artistic dictator.

The fall of Robespierre in 1794 nearly cost him his head. He was imprisoned twice, and it was behind bars that he painted his haunted self-portrait. He survived, and found a new master in the rising general Napoleon Bonaparte, whom he served as First Painter to the Emperor, glorifying him on horseback in the Alps and in the vast pageantry of the Coronation.

When Napoleon fell and the Bourbons returned in 1816, David — a regicide who had voted to kill their brother — was driven into exile. He settled in Brussels, honored abroad but forbidden to come home. He went on painting mythological subjects, taught a generation of pupils, and died there in 1825 at seventy-seven. France refused to let his body be buried on its soil. The painter who had crowned an emperor and mourned a revolution's dead was laid to rest in a foreign city.
