---
name: "Impressionism"
order: 8
years: "c. 1860–1890"
---

Paris was being torn down and rebuilt around them — Haussmann's boulevards slicing through the medieval city, gaslight, railway stations, café terraces full of strangers. A generation of young painters decided this glittering, restless modern life was worth painting, and the official Salon decided it absolutely was not. Rejected year after year, ridiculed by critics, Monet, Renoir, Degas, Pissarro and their friends finally staged their own exhibition in 1874 in a photographer's abandoned studio. A mocking reviewer seized on the title of one Monet canvas — Impression, Sunrise — and sneered that these were mere "impressionists." The insult became a banner. They carried their easels outdoors, chased light as it changed by the minute, and painted with quick, broken strokes that looked like chaos up close and like life itself at ten paces. They were laughed at, they were broke — Monet at his lowest begged friends for bread money — and within a generation they had permanently changed what the world expects a painting to be.
